Executive Summary

Five things that matter

Every figure below comes from the production database, not from an estimate. Where I could not get data, I have said so explicitly rather than filling the gap.

1

Publishing has all but stopped, and the audience followed

Content output fell roughly 91% between 2023 and 2026. Newsletter sign-ups fell in lockstep: August 2025 to August 2026 is a 69% year-on-year decline. This is the single clearest cause-and-effect in the data.

2

Four templates are quietly doing a third of the work

Four downloadable templates, published once in 2023 and never touched since, account for roughly a third of every form submission the site has ever received — and one of them took a submission on the day I pulled this data.

3

The demand pattern is independently confirmed

MTN's four best-performing templates match SME South Africa's most-downloaded resources in almost exactly the same order, across two unrelated audiences. This is structural SME demand, not an audience quirk — which makes it safe to build on.

4

Nothing on the site is set up to measure a conversion

Analytics is collecting traffic but has no conversion tracking configured at all — no events on a template download, no event on a newsletter sign-up. Template downloads and sign-ups, the two things that actually work, are invisible to every report we can pull.

5

There is real, untapped organic search demand sitting on pages that rank poorly

Search Console access came through this week. Two funding-related guides are pulling in 10,000+ and 14,000+ impressions over the last 28 days each, but sit at positions 39–44 — meaning the demand exists and Google is showing the page, but it isn't winning the click. That is a content/SEO fix, not a rebuild, and it is available immediately.

What is not working

Structure, measurement and hygiene

1. Navigation does not match the content

This is the highest-value fix, and the mockup already addresses it. The site holds 90 articles organised into meaningful pillars, but the live navigation offers only Home, Business Advice and Insights. The structure exists in the database and is invisible to the visitor.

PillarArticlesAssessment
Connect My Business33Strong
Manage My Business32Strong
Build My Business22Strong
Ecommerce7Thin for a named pillar
Feature My Business2A pillar with no content

"Feature My Business" promises SME spotlights and delivers two articles. Either commit to it as a content stream — and it is a good one, because featured SMEs promote their own feature — or fold it away until there is substance behind it.

2. The category system is genuinely broken

There are two category taxonomies, and one is a typo
Alongside the working business-advise-category there is a second taxonomy spelled business-advicse-category, containing 14 near-duplicate terms — its own "Connect My Business", "Build My Business", "Finance" and so on. Two articles are filed under the misspelt one, where nothing will ever find them. It needs merging and deleting, carefully, with redirects.

Two further empty taxonomies exist: insight-category has six terms and zero articles assigned — so "Insights" appears in the navigation while its category system is entirely unused. sme-guides-categories is similarly empty.

There is also no hierarchy: the four strategic pillars sit in the same flat list as eleven ad-hoc topics (Entrepreneurship, Finance, Technology, Human Resources, Automation, Modernisation, Customer Retention and others). An article can be tagged "Connect My Business" and "Finance" with nothing to indicate one is a pillar and the other a topic. That is why the navigation could never be generated automatically from the content.

3. Nothing measures a conversion

Analytics is installed via Site Kit and collecting page views. Beyond that, the configuration is empty. This is a pure organic site — no paid campaigns, no ad platforms in the mix — so the fix is entirely within GA4 itself:

The funnel was genuinely converting at around 11% and every report believed it converted at zero. MTN's hub is in the same position: the two things that demonstrably work — templates and sign-ups — are the two things no report can see.

The redesign

The idea behind the mockup

The brief asked for elevated presentation of the same content. The mockup delivers that, but its more important move is structural: it introduces a commercial path the current site simply does not have. Here is the reasoning behind each decision.

Move 1 — Make the navigation reflect the content that exists

A mega-menu exposes all five pillars directly, each with a plain-language descriptor ("Connect My Business — websites, funding, going online"). Nothing new has to be written for this to work: 87 of the 90 existing articles already sit in the three strong pillars. This converts a hidden archive into a browsable library on day one, and it is the cheapest meaningful win available.

Move 2 — Give "Insights" something to actually be

Today Insights is a nav item with an empty taxonomy behind it. The mockup splits it into Guides, Templates, Learning & Development and Events. Two consequences worth noting: Templates get promoted to a first-class destination — the best-converting content on the site is currently not linked from the main navigation at all — and Events finally has a home, which is the nudge needed to publish those fourteen drafts.

Move 3 — Add Business Solutions as a top-level destination

This is the pivot. The hub currently talks about business problems and stops there. The mockup adds a product layer carrying the real MTN Business catalogue — Made For Business voice and data plans, Managed Firewall, SOC as a Service — so an SME reading about connectivity or cybersecurity has somewhere to go that is a product rather than another article.

Move 4 — The plan configurator, which is where leads are actually made

The configurator page is the most commercially significant screen in the set, and it is deliberately modelled on how MTN Business already sells:

ElementPurpose
Contract term toggle (24 / 36 months)Captures commercial intent, and changes the monthly figure live.
Device add-on with quantity stepperTurns a plan enquiry into a sized deal. Quantity is a strong qualification signal — it tells a consultant the size of the business before they call.
Live receipt and monthly totalRemoves the uncertainty that makes SMEs abandon telco enquiries.
Two blocking consent checkboxesApply stays disabled until both are ticked. POPIA consent captured at source, not assumed.
"No payment is taken on this site"Sets expectation honestly — this is a qualified lead handover, not e-commerce.
Reference number on completionGives the SME something to quote, and the consultant something to look up. This is the field the API fills.

The sequence is the point: content earns the attention, the template captures the identity, the configurator captures the intent. A template download tells you a business exists and what it is worried about. A completed configuration tells you what it wants to buy, for how long, and at what scale. The first is a contact; the second is a lead a consultant can act on immediately.

Three things to fix in the mockup before it is shown to MTN
1. Invented statistics. The lead-gen callout claims "4,200+ SMEs assisted this year" and a "15 min average callback time". Both are placeholders. Replace with real MTN figures or delete them — unverifiable numbers in a telco's own brand are a genuine risk, not a rounding error.

2. The corporate typeface. The mockup renders in Inter because MTN Brighter Sans is a licensed font that is not web-available to us. This is a reasonable stand-in, but MTN should either supply the licensed web font files or formally approve the substitute before launch.

3. Plan pricing is hardcoded. The page carries its own honest disclaimer — "confirm current terms with MTN Business before publishing live". That disclaimer is exactly the argument for the pricing API in the next section.
The lead engine

API integration strategy

MTN Business already sells these products through its own channels. The hub should not rebuild that — it should feed it. The integration has four surfaces, and they are worth separating because they carry very different dependencies, risks and delivery timelines.

Scope note
Our current engagement on this site is organic — publishing content and managing the newsletter, no paid media of any kind. Everything below is proposed forward strategy, not work already in scope, and none of it depends on or assumes any ad spend. Phase 0 and 1 need nothing from MTN beyond a conversation and content sign-off; phases 3 and 4 (the lead API itself) would be a separate scope conversation once MTN Business confirms what it can expose.
What happens without the API
Prices are typed into WordPress by hand and drift out of date — a telco advertising a price it no longer offers. Leads land in a WordPress table and someone re-keys them into MTN's sales system, losing hours and dropping records. And because the handover breaks the data chain, nobody can ever prove which article produced which sale — which is precisely the number that renews a sponsorship budget.
1

Catalogue & pricing sync Inbound · scheduled

Pull the live product and pricing catalogue from MTN Business on a schedule rather than maintaining it by hand.

  • Store as a product post type with structured fields — the site already runs ACF Pro and Custom Post Type UI, so no new stack is needed
  • Serve every page from the local copy, so page speed never depends on MTN's API responding
  • Stamp each price with an "as at" date so the source of truth is visible
  • Fail safe: if a sync fails, keep serving the last known-good catalogue and raise an alert — never show a blank or a zero price
  • Retires the "confirm terms before publishing" risk permanently
2

Lead submission Outbound · real time

The core of the engine. When an SME completes a configuration, the lead reaches a consultant's queue in seconds with the full configuration attached.

  • Write locally first, then transmit. The lead is committed to our own database before any external call. A lead is never lost because someone else's API was down
  • Idempotency key per submission so a retry can never create a duplicate lead in MTN's CRM
  • Queue with backoff retry — an MTN-side outage delays delivery, it does not destroy it
  • Store the returned case reference and show it to the SME — the reference field already in the mockup
  • Alert on repeated failure so a silent breakage cannot run for weeks
3

Status callback Inbound · webhook

MTN Business posts lead progress back to the hub: contacted, qualified, quoted, won, lost.

  • Signed requests (HMAC) plus IP allow-listing — this endpoint accepts commercial data and must be locked down
  • Updates the local lead record to close the loop
  • This is the difference between "the hub produced 200 leads" and "the hub produced R-value in contracted revenue." Only the second one justifies a budget
  • If MTN cannot push, a scheduled pull against the reference IDs achieves the same result more slowly
4

Conversion reporting Outbound · analytics

Feed real outcomes back into GA4 so the content team — not an ad platform — can see what is actually working.

  • Fire a qualified-lead event with plan tier and value — only on a genuinely complete submission, never on a page view
  • Configure the two conversion events this report already flags as missing: template download and newsletter sign-up
  • Once a lead is marked won, log that outcome against the stored content path so we can say which article, template or guide it started from
  • This is a pure organic reporting loop — no ad platform is involved anywhere in this stack

The lead payload — what a consultant actually receives

Shape matters more than syntax here. The attribution block is the part that is usually left out and is the reason most content-to-sales handovers can never be measured.

// Illustrative structure — field names to be agreed with MTN Business
{
  "idempotency_key": "hub-2026-08-28-a91f3c",   // retry-safe, no duplicates
  "contact":  { name, business_name, phone, email, province },
  "configuration": {
     "product_sku": "MFB-L",               // from the synced catalogue
     "term_months": 36,
     "device_required": true,
     "device_quantity": 4,                 // implies business size
     "quoted_monthly": 499
  },
  "consent": {
     "privacy_accepted_at": "2026-08-28T09:14:22+02:00",
     "terms_accepted_at":   "2026-08-28T09:14:24+02:00",
     "wording_version":     "v2.1"        // POPIA: prove what they agreed to
  },
  "attribution": {                          // the organic content case lives here
     "first_landing_page": "/business-advice/what-are-working-capital-loans",
     "content_path": ["article:working-capital", "template:cash-flow"],
     "referral_source": "organic_search",        // or newsletter, or direct
     "days_from_first_visit": 6
  }
}

Attribution is what turns the hub into a business case

Carrying that attribution block end to end lets MTN answer questions it currently cannot ask: which article produces the most Managed Firewall deals? Does a cash-flow template download predict a larger plan? How long does an SME take from first read to signed contract? Those answers reframe the hub from a marketing cost into a measurable acquisition channel with a cost per acquired customer.

The template download is the pivotal moment in that chain — it is where an anonymous reader becomes a named business with a known problem. That is why expanding the template library is both the content recommendation and the lead-generation recommendation. They are the same recommendation.

Data protection

Consent is captured at source with a timestamp and the version of the wording agreed to, and travels with the lead. Only what MTN Business needs in order to make contact and quote is transmitted. Credentials live in server configuration, never in the repository and never in browser code. A retention period and deletion path are defined before launch, not after. The mockup's blocking consent checkboxes are already the correct pattern.

Already in production

We have built this exact architecture

On SME South Africa we run server-to-server lead submission into a funding partner's API — local-first write, retry queue, returned reference IDs. It removed the manual re-keying step and guaranteed attribution. This is not a theoretical design.

Already in production

And the closed-loop tracking

We log partner conversion callbacks server-side, and this month wired a qualified-lead conversion signal that only fires when a submission meets a real commercial bar — entirely organic, no ad platform involved. Steps 3 and 4 above are proven patterns for us.

Delivery

Sequenced so value lands before the hard dependencies

The integration depends on MTN Business, and that dependency is outside our control. The order below is deliberately arranged so the work that needs nothing from MTN happens first.

PHASE 0
Dependency

Establish what MTN Business can actually expose

Not a build step — a conversation. Everything in phases 2 to 4 is shaped by the answers. Start it immediately and in parallel with phase 1, because it will take the longest and is the critical path.

PHASE 1
Do first

Content, structure and measurement — zero external dependencies

Merge the duplicate taxonomy and redirect. Introduce the pillar hierarchy. Ship the category-aware navigation. Promote Templates into the main nav. Publish the fourteen draft events. Configure GA4 conversion events for template downloads and newsletter sign-ups. Refresh the two high-impression, low-ranking funding guides flagged in the search demand section. Disable the theme editor, retire the dead form stack. Every one of these is available now, needs no ad spend, and several are the highest-return items in this report.

PHASE 2
Do first

Expand the template library

The clearest evidence-backed action available. Four templates from 2023 drive a third of all conversions, and our own library confirms the same demand pattern with a longer list of proven titles — income statement, breakeven calculator, business budget, quotation, startup checklist. This needs no API and no MTN sign-off beyond content approval.

PHASE 3
On MTN readiness

Lead submission API and the configurator

Build the configurator against the synced catalogue, wire outbound lead submission with local-first durability and idempotency, surface real reference numbers. Ship behind a feature flag so the redesign can launch before the integration is signed off.

PHASE 4
On MTN readiness

Closed-loop reporting

Status webhook, won/lost outcomes flowing back, and a reporting view that shows content-to-revenue inside GA4 and our own dashboards. No ad platform is involved at any point — this is the phase that makes the hub defensible at budget time on its own organic merits.

Phase 0

What we need to ask MTN Business

These determine the shape of the integration. I would put them in writing before committing to any delivery dates on phases 3 and 4.

Is there a lead-intake API, and which system sits behind it?
Salesforce, Dynamics, Siebel or something in-house — each has a different integration pattern and a different effort profile.
Is there a product and pricing API, or is the catalogue maintained manually?
If manual, phase 1 needs an interim process for keeping prices honest, and a visible "as at" date becomes mandatory rather than nice to have.
What is the authentication model?
OAuth2 client credentials, mutual TLS, or a static API key. This drives credential storage, rotation and the security review.
Can MTN push status updates, or must we poll?
Push gives near-real-time closed-loop reporting. Polling works, more slowly, and needs rate limits agreed.
Is there a sandbox?
We will not test lead submission against a live sales queue. Without a sandbox the integration timeline lengthens materially.
How should we handle an SME who is already an MTN customer?
Deduplication rules, and whether an existing customer should be routed to account management instead of new business.
What are the rate limits and the support SLA?
Determines queue design, and who is called when leads stop arriving at 5pm on a Friday.
Who owns the lead once it is delivered?
Needs to be explicit for POPIA, and for reporting: if MTN never reports an outcome, the hub can never demonstrate its value.
The argument

In one paragraph

MTN SME Hub has 90 genuinely useful articles, four templates that still convert three years after publication, and a demand pattern independently confirmed by a second SME audience. What it lacks is supply and structure: publishing has fallen by roughly 91%, the navigation hides the content, and nothing on the site counts a conversion — even though real organic search demand is sitting untapped on pages Google already shows tens of thousands of times a month. The redesign fixes the structure. Restarting content fixes the supply. Configuring GA4 properly fixes the measurement. All of it is achievable within our current organic scope — publishing and newsletter management — with no paid media anywhere in the plan. The API integration is a separate, larger opportunity worth proposing once MTN Business confirms what it can expose.
