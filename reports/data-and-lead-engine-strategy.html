MTN SME Hub: a data review and a plan for turning it into a lead engine

A review of the hub's content, search performance and newsletter, and a proposed path for connecting it to MTN Business as a measurable source of leads.

Site, search and newsletter data
Executive Summary

Six findings

Every figure below comes from the site's own database, its Search Console account, or its newsletter platform. Where a data source was not yet available, that is stated rather than estimated.

1

Publishing has almost stopped, and both the audience and the newsletter followed

Content output fell by roughly 91% between 2023 and 2026. Newsletter sign-ups fell over the same period by 69% year on year. This is the clearest cause and effect in the data: less content, fewer sign-ups.

2

Four templates are doing a third of the work

Four downloadable templates, published once in 2023 and never updated since, account for roughly a third of every form submission the site has ever received. Two of them produced a new lead within 48 hours of this data being pulled.

3

The demand pattern is confirmed by a second, unrelated audience

MTN's four best-performing templates match SME South Africa's own most-downloaded resources in almost exactly the same order, across two audiences with no overlap. This is how South African small businesses behave, not a quirk of MTN's traffic.

4

The newsletter's engagement has declined steadily since it launched

Average open rate has fallen from the low sixties in the first months to the low twenties more recently, and click rate has fallen in step. The list has grown in size while engagement per send has gone the other way.

5

Nothing on the site currently counts a conversion

Analytics is collecting page views but has no conversion tracking configured. A template download and a newsletter sign-up, the two things that demonstrably work, are invisible to every report the site produces.

6

Real search demand is sitting on pages that rank too low to be found

Two funding-related guides receive over 10,000 and 14,000 impressions each in Google over a 28-day period, but sit at positions 39 to 44, well past where people click. The demand already exists. The content just needs a refresh to compete for it.

Recipients per send grew from around 770 to close to 2,000 over the period covered. Open rate fell from an average of 45.5% across the first six sends to 20.0% across the last six, and click rate fell from 4.5% to 1.6% over the same comparison. The redesign

The thinking behind the mockup

The brief called for a more polished presentation of the same content. The mockup delivers that, and its larger contribution is structural: it introduces a commercial path the current site does not have. The reasoning behind each decision follows.

Move 1: make the navigation reflect the content that exists

A mega-menu exposes all five pillars directly, each with a plain-language descriptor ("Connect My Business: websites, funding, going online"). Nothing new needs to be written for this to work. 87 of the 90 existing articles already sit in the three strong pillars. This turns a hidden archive into a browsable library from day one, and is the least costly meaningful win available.

Move 2: give "Insights" a clear purpose

Today Insights is a navigation item sitting over an unused category system. The mockup splits it into Guides, Templates, Learning and Development, and Events. Templates move to a first-class destination, since the best-converting content on the site is not currently linked from the main navigation at all, and Events has a home, which is the push needed to publish the fourteen drafts.

Move 3: add Business Solutions as a top-level destination

This is the pivot point. The hub currently discusses business problems and stops there. The mockup adds a product layer carrying the real MTN Business catalogue: Made For Business voice and data plans, Managed Firewall, SOC as a Service, so a reader looking at an article on connectivity or cybersecurity has somewhere to go that offers a product, not another article.

Move 4: the plan configurator, where leads are actually made

The configurator page is the most commercially significant screen in the mockup, modelled on how MTN Business already sells:

ElementPurpose
Contract term toggle (24 / 36 months)Captures commercial intent and updates the monthly figure live.
Device add-on with quantity stepperTurns a plan enquiry into a sized deal. Quantity signals the size of the business before a consultant makes contact.
Live receipt and monthly totalRemoves the uncertainty that leads SMEs to abandon telco enquiries.
Two blocking consent checkboxesThe apply action stays disabled until both are ticked, so POPIA consent is captured at source.
"No payment is taken on this site"Sets expectations honestly. This is a qualified lead handover, not e-commerce.
Reference number on completionGives the SME something to quote and the consultant something to look up.

The sequence matters: content earns attention, the template captures identity, the configurator captures intent. A template download tells you a business exists and what it is concerned about. A completed configuration tells you what it wants to buy, for how long, and at what scale. The first is a contact. The second is a lead a consultant can act on immediately.

The lead engine

A proposed integration with MTN Business

MTN Business already sells these products through its own channels. The hub should feed that process rather than rebuild it. What follows is a proposed direction for discussion, built around four surfaces with different dependencies and timelines.

The cost of leaving this unconnected
Prices typed into the site by hand drift out of date. Leads land in a site database and someone has to re-key them into MTN's sales system by hand, which loses time and drops records. Because that handover breaks the chain of information, nobody can trace which article led to which sale, which is the number that justifies continued investment in the hub.
1

Catalogue and pricing sync Inbound, scheduled

Pull the live product and pricing catalogue from MTN Business on a schedule rather than maintaining it by hand.

  • Store it as structured content on the site, using the tools already in place. No new platform is needed
  • Serve every page from the local copy, so page speed never depends on an external system responding
  • Stamp each price with an "as at" date so the source is clear
  • If a sync fails, keep serving the last known-good catalogue and raise an alert rather than showing a blank or a zero price
2

Lead submission Outbound, real time

The core of the engine. When an SME completes a configuration, the lead reaches a consultant's queue within seconds, with the full configuration attached.

  • The lead is written to the site's own database first, then transmitted, so it is never lost if MTN's system is briefly unavailable
  • A unique key per submission means a retry can never create a duplicate lead
  • Failed transmissions queue and retry automatically, so an outage delays delivery rather than losing it
  • The returned case reference is shown to the SME, using the field already present in the mockup
3

Status callback Inbound, webhook

MTN Business posts lead progress back to the hub: contacted, qualified, quoted, won, lost.

  • Signed requests and IP restrictions, since this endpoint accepts commercial data
  • Updates the local lead record so the loop closes on both sides
  • This is what turns "the hub produced 200 leads" into "the hub produced this much contracted revenue," the number that justifies a budget
  • If MTN cannot push updates, a scheduled check against reference numbers achieves the same result more slowly
4

Conversion reporting Outbound, reporting

Feed real outcomes into the site's own analytics so results are visible without relying on a separate platform.

  • Log a qualified-lead event with plan tier and value, only once a submission is fully complete
  • Configure the two conversion events flagged earlier as missing: template download and newsletter sign-up
  • Once a lead is marked won, record that outcome against the content it started from, so the site can say which article, template or guide led to it

The lead payload: what a consultant would receive

The shape of the data matters more than the exact syntax here. The attribution block is the part most often left out, and the reason most handovers from content to sales cannot be measured afterward.

     "terms_accepted_at":   "2026-08-28T09:14:24+02:00",

  },
  "attribution": {                          // the content case lives here

     "content_path": ["article:working-capital", "template:cash-flow"],
     "referral_source": "organic_search",        // or newsletter, or direct
     "days_from_first_visit": 6
  }
}

Attribution turns the hub into a business case

Carrying that attribution block through end to end lets MTN answer questions it cannot currently ask: which article produces the most Managed Firewall enquiries, whether a cash-flow template download predicts a larger plan, and how long an SME typically takes from first read to signed contract. Those answers reframe the hub from a cost centre into a channel with a measurable cost per customer acquired.

The template download is the key moment in that chain, where an anonymous reader becomes a named business with a known need. That is why expanding the template library serves both the content goal and the lead-generation goal at once.

Data protection

Consent would be captured at source with a timestamp and the wording version agreed to, and would travel with the lead. Only what MTN Business needs to make contact and quote would be transmitted. Credentials would live in server configuration, not in code. A retention period and deletion path would be defined before launch. The mockup's blocking consent checkboxes already reflect this approach.

Proven pattern

This architecture is already running elsewhere

A comparable server-to-server lead submission into a funding partner's system is already live on a related platform: local-first write, retry queue, returned reference numbers. It removed manual re-keying and guaranteed attribution. This is a working design, not a proposal on paper only.

Proven pattern

The closed-loop reporting side too

Partner conversion callbacks are logged in the same way, and a qualified-lead signal that only fires on a fully complete submission is already in production. Steps 3 and 4 above follow patterns already tested and working.

Delivery

A sequence that delivers value before the harder dependencies

The integration depends on MTN Business, and that dependency sits outside this team's control. The order below puts the work that needs nothing from MTN first.

PHASE 0
Dependency

Establish what MTN Business can expose

Not a build step. A conversation. Everything in phases 2 to 4 depends on the answers, so it is worth starting alongside phase 1 rather than after it, since it will likely take the longest.

PHASE 1
Do first

Content, structure and measurement, no external dependencies

Merge the duplicate category system and redirect. Introduce the pillar hierarchy. Ship the category-aware navigation. Promote Templates into the main navigation. Publish the fourteen draft events. Configure conversion events for template downloads and newsletter sign-ups. Refresh the two high-impression, low-ranking funding guides identified in the search demand section. Disable the theme editor and retire the unused form system. Every item here is available now, and several are the highest-return actions in this report.

PHASE 2
Do first

Expand the template library

The clearest evidence-backed action available. Four templates from 2023 already drive a third of all conversions, and a comparable library confirms the same demand pattern with further proven titles: income statement, breakeven calculator, business budget, quotation, and a startup checklist. This needs no API and no sign-off beyond content approval.

PHASE 3
On MTN readiness

Lead submission API and the configurator

Build the configurator against the synced catalogue, connect outbound lead submission with local-first durability, and surface real reference numbers. This can ship behind a feature flag so the redesign can launch before the integration is signed off.

PHASE 4
On MTN readiness

Closed-loop reporting

Status webhook, won and lost outcomes flowing back, and a reporting view that shows content-to-revenue. This is the phase that makes the case for the hub's ongoing value in concrete terms.

Phase 0

Questions for MTN Business

These shape the integration, and are worth confirming in writing before committing to delivery dates on phases 3 and 4.

Is there a lead-intake API, and which system sits behind it?
Salesforce, Dynamics, an in-house system, or something else. Each has a different integration pattern and effort profile.
Is there a product and pricing API, or is the catalogue maintained manually?
If manual, phase 1 needs an interim process for keeping prices current, and a visible "as at" date becomes necessary rather than optional.
What is the authentication model?
OAuth2 client credentials, mutual TLS, or a static API key. This shapes credential storage, rotation and the security review.
Can MTN push status updates, or does the hub need to poll?
Push allows near real-time closed-loop reporting. Polling works, more slowly, and needs rate limits agreed in advance.
Is a sandbox available?
Testing lead submission against a live sales queue is not advisable. Without a sandbox, the integration timeline extends.
How should an SME who is already an MTN customer be handled?
Deduplication rules, and whether an existing customer should route to account management instead of new business.
What are the rate limits and the support arrangement?
Shapes queue design, and who is contacted if leads stop arriving outside business hours.
In summary

Where this leaves the hub

MTN SME Hub has 90 useful articles, four templates that still convert three years after publication, and a demand pattern confirmed by a second, unrelated audience. What it lacks is supply and structure: publishing has fallen by roughly 91%, the navigation hides the content that exists, the newsletter's engagement has declined as the content behind it slowed, and nothing on the site counts a conversion, even while real search demand sits untapped on pages Google already shows tens of thousands of times a month. The redesign addresses the structure. Restarting content addresses the supply. Proper conversion tracking addresses the measurement. The proposed API integration is a further opportunity worth exploring once MTN Business confirms what it can expose.
