Complete Guide to Registering a Business in South Africa

Last updated 26 August 2026

Registering a business in South Africa is more straightforward than most founders expect, but the order you do things in matters. Skip a step, or do them out of sequence, and you can end up waiting weeks for a tax number that should have taken days. This guide walks through the full process, from reserving your company name through to registering as an employer, in the order the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) and SARS actually expect.

Before you start, it helps to know roughly what you're getting into. Most straightforward registrations, where the applicant's documents are in order and the chosen name is approved on the first attempt, take between one and two weeks from start to finish and cost in the region of R175 in CIPC fees. The steps below cover what happens at each stage, what you'll need to have ready, and where founders most commonly lose time.

What you'll need before you start
  • · Your South African ID, or a passport if you're a non-citizen
  • · A proposed company name, plus two or three backup options
  • · A registered address for the business
  • · Around R175 in fees and five to ten working days

1 Reserve your company name

The first step is submitting your proposed company name to CIPC for approval, which you can do directly through the CIPC online portal. You're allowed to submit up to four name options in a single application, and it's worth using all four slots. Names are checked against the existing companies register for duplicates, and against a list of restricted or reserved words (terms implying a government or professional affiliation you don't have, for example), so a name that seems obviously available can still be rejected.

Name reservation is usually the fastest part of the whole process, typically resolved within one to two working days, and once approved, your reserved name is held for you for six months while you complete the rest of registration.

2 Choose your business structure

Most new South African SMEs register as a Private Company, commonly written as (Pty) Ltd. This structure limits your personal liability to what you've invested in the business, separates your personal and business assets legally, and is the structure most banks, funders and larger clients expect to see when they do their own due diligence on you.

A sole proprietorship is simpler to set up and has fewer ongoing compliance requirements, but it offers no separation between you and the business: if the business owes money or is sued, your personal assets are exposed. Unless you have a specific reason not to, registering as a Pty Ltd from the outset saves you from having to convert your business structure later, which is a more involved process than registering correctly the first time.

Pro tip

Almost all SME funders, including most grant and enterprise development programmes, only accept applications from Pty Ltd companies. If funding is anywhere on your roadmap, even a year or two out, register Pty Ltd now rather than converting later.

3 Register with CIPC

With your name approved and structure decided, you file the actual company registration through the CIPC online portal. You'll need your approved name reservation reference, certified copies of your ID (or the IDs of all directors, if there's more than one), and the registered physical address the business will operate from.

Standard processing runs five to ten working days once your application is submitted correctly. Applications are commonly delayed by uncertified ID copies, mismatched details between the name reservation and the registration form, or an incomplete registered address, so it's worth double-checking each of these before you submit.

  • Certified ID copy for every director, uploaded in the correct format
  • Registration fee paid and the payment reference kept on file

4 Get your registration certificate and number

Once CIPC approves your application, you'll receive a Certificate of Incorporation along with a unique company registration number. This is the document that proves your business legally exists, and you'll be asked to produce it, or the registration number, at almost every subsequent step: opening a bank account, registering for tax, applying for funding, and signing contracts with larger clients.

Keep both a digital and a printed copy somewhere secure. It's common to need the original or a certified copy again months later for something unrelated to registration, like a tender application.

5 Register for tax with SARS

In most cases, successful CIPC registration automatically triggers the creation of an income tax reference number with SARS, and you'll be able to see this reflected on eFiling within a few working days of your CIPC certificate being issued. It's worth logging into eFiling yourself to confirm this has happened rather than waiting for a letter, since the automatic sync between CIPC and SARS occasionally fails to go through.

VAT registration is a separate, later step. It becomes compulsory once your business's turnover exceeds R1 million in any consecutive twelve-month period, though you can register voluntarily before that if it suits your business, for example if most of your clients are VAT-registered businesses that can claim the input tax back.

Pro tip

Don't wait for a letter from SARS to confirm you have a tax number. Log into eFiling directly the day after your CIPC certificate is issued and search for your company. Catching a failed sync early can save you several weeks compared to discovering it only once you need the number urgently.

6 Open a business bank account

With your Certificate of Incorporation, ID, and proof of the registered address in hand, you can open a dedicated business bank account. Most South African banks offer a business account tier aimed specifically at new small companies, often with reduced fees for the first year.

Keeping business banking entirely separate from your personal accounts from day one is worth the small amount of extra admin it involves. It makes your bookkeeping dramatically simpler, it's what accountants and auditors expect to see, and it's frequently a hard requirement for funding applications and larger client contracts later on.

7 Register as an employer, if you're hiring

If you plan to bring on staff, even a single employee, you need to register for PAYE (Pay As You Earn) and UIF (Unemployment Insurance Fund) with SARS before their first payday. This is a separate registration from your company's income tax registration, and it needs to be in place before you run your first payroll, not after.

If payroll compliance feels like a lot to take on alongside everything else involved in getting a new business off the ground, our guide on setting up payroll for the first time walks through PAYE, UIF and the Skills Development Levy in more detail.

Frequently asked questions

How long does business registration actually take?
Name reservation is usually same-day to two working days. CIPC company registration itself takes five to ten working days once submitted correctly. Budget around two weeks in total if this is your first time going through the process.
Do I need a lawyer or accountant to register?
No. CIPC registration is designed to be done directly by the founder through the online portal. An accountant becomes genuinely useful once you're dealing with VAT registration, payroll, or more complex shareholding structures involving multiple directors.
Can I register a business from home, without commercial premises?
Yes. CIPC only requires a registered address where official correspondence can reach you, and your home address is acceptable for this. You can update your registered address later at no cost if the business moves into commercial premises.
What happens if my chosen name gets rejected?
CIPC will automatically move on to your second or third choice if you provided backup names in the same application. If all of your submitted names are rejected, you'll need to resubmit a fresh application, which typically adds a few more working days to the process.
