What Are Working Capital Loans?

MTN SME Hub Editorial
· 12 Aug 2026 · 6 min read

If you're looking for a solution to help cover your business's regular operational expenses, you can look into working capital loans. Working capital loans are short-term loans businesses typically take when there's a shortfall in their working capital. These loans help cover the day-to-day running costs of a business, not long-term investments or major equipment upgrades.

Instead of pausing operations during tough months or waiting for invoices to clear, a working capital loan allows you to stay on top of business needs without dipping into emergency reserves or selling off assets.

Why Businesses Take Working Capital Loans

Businesses typically don't fail because of a bad product; in reality, businesses are crippled by cash flow mistakes. You can have invoices lined up and loyal customers, but if you can't cover monthly salaries, pay rent, or restock inventory, you'll feel the pressure quickly.

  • Manage slow business seasons (e.g., after the festive period or winter dip)
  • Buy stock ahead of a high-sales period
  • Cover payroll while waiting on receivables
  • Finance short-term projects or campaigns
  • Pay suppliers to keep the supply chain running smoothly

How Do Working Capital Loans Work?

Picture this: you run a small Shisa Nyama in the township. During summer weekends, business is booming. Meat is flying off the grill, you've got regulars, and cash flow is healthy. But come winter, foot traffic slows. Your suppliers still need to be paid, staff still need salaries, and you want to stay open for the customers who do still come.

A working capital loan can step in to fill that temporary gap and help maintain a healthy cash flow during those slow seasons, covering your monthly stock order or funding a winter special to keep the regulars coming.

Types of Working Capital Loans

Secured working capital: backed by collateral such as accounts receivable, inventory, or equipment. Generally offers lower interest rates and more flexible terms due to reduced lender risk.

Unsecured working capital: does not require collateral, relying instead on creditworthiness and financial history. Faster approval, but typically higher interest rates.

Advantages & Disadvantages

  • Speed and flexibility: quicker to access than traditional business loans
  • Cash flow relief without depleting reserves
  • No equity dilution: you keep full control of your business
  • Short repayment windows can put pressure on cash flow if not well-planned
  • Higher interest rates than long-term loans

Where to Get Working Capital Loans in South Africa

A few reliable providers include Growise Capital, Get Funds, and Betterbanc. Before you take out a business loan, ensure you have a clear plan. Forecast your income and expenses, and build a repayment strategy before the funds hit your account.
